A: The primary purpose of a space suit, also known as an Extravehicular Activity (EVA) suit, is to provide a safe and reliable environment for astronauts to work outside their spacecraft or space station. The suit protects astronauts from the harsh conditions of space, including extreme temperatures, lack of oxygen, and radiation. It maintains a comfortable internal temperature, around 20°C, and humidity level, while also supplying oxygen for the astronaut to breathe. The suit is pressurized to about 0.3 times the atmospheric pressure on Earth, which is roughly the pressure at an altitude of 3,000 metres. This pressurization helps to prevent the astronaut's body fluids from boiling due to the low external pressure.

Q: How do space suits regulate temperature, and what materials are used to achieve this?
A: Space suits have multiple layers, including a thermal insulation layer, to regulate temperature. The thermal insulation layer is made of materials such as polyester or nylon, which provide excellent thermal insulation properties. The suit also has a cooling system, known as the Liquid Cooling and Ventilation Garment (LCVG), which circulates cooled water through tubes to keep the astronaut cool. In addition, the suit has a outer layer that reflects sunlight and helps to maintain a stable internal temperature. This multi-layered approach helps to maintain a comfortable temperature for the astronaut, even in extreme external conditions.

Q: What is the significance of the life support system in a space suit, and how does it work?
A: The life support system in a space suit is crucial for sustaining the astronaut's life during an EVA. The system provides oxygen for the astronaut to breathe, removes carbon dioxide, and regulates the suit's temperature and humidity. The system consists of a oxygen supply tank, a carbon dioxide scrubber, and a ventilation system. The oxygen supply tank provides a limited supply of oxygen, which is sufficient for the duration of the EVA. The carbon dioxide scrubber removes carbon dioxide from the astronaut's exhaled breath, and the ventilation system circulates the air within the suit to prevent moisture buildup.
