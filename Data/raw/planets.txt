

Q: What is unique about the atmosphere on Venus, and how does it relate to acid rain?
A: The atmosphere on Venus is known for being incredibly dense and hostile, composed mainly of carbon dioxide, with clouds of sulfuric acid droplets. The sulfuric acid in the clouds is what leads to the phenomenon of acid rain on Venus, although it evaporates before reaching the surface due to the extreme heat. This atmosphere is of great interest to scientists studying extreme climates and potential implications for Earth's own atmospheric conditions. The study of Venus' atmosphere also helps in understanding how planets can evolve differently from Earth.
